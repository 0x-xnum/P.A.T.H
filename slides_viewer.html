WHY IS THE LUNAR SOUTH POLE SO COMPLICATED?

NASA Engineers Simulating Artemis III Lighting
Testing horizontal grazing light at NASA Johnson Space Center (Photo ID: CEB_1528). At low solar angles, even tiny rocks cast massive deceptive shadows.
4 KM CRATER RIMS → 50+ KM SHADOWS

Because the Sun never climbs above 2°, crater rims cast shadows stretching dozens of kilometers. Landing inside an unexpected shadow means zero solar power and immediate battery exhaustion.

1.54° AXIAL TILT • THE SKY NEVER REPEATS

The Moon's tilt changes solar elevation across seasons, while Earth wobbles by ±7° in the sky. A site with 100% sunlight in December can be plunged into pitch darkness by June.

THE PLANNING GAP: EXISTING TOOLS ARE STATIC AND HEAVY

Flight controllers rely on multi-gigabyte SPICE kernels and offline batch simulations. If a launch slips by just 24 hours, planners have NO real-time interactive tool to test the new landing window on the fly.
